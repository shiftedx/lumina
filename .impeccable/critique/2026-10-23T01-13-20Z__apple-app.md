# Lumina native UI: media-first design audit

Scope: current iOS and tvOS SwiftUI sources in `apple/App`, reviewed against PRODUCT.md and the user's direction: let media lead; keep controls quiet and out of the way. Independent design review completed before detector results were considered. Historical user screenshots provide context but do not establish the appearance of the current unbuilt code.

## Verdict

The artwork foundation fits the product. Control hierarchy still needs restraint, particularly the newly added playback actions and settings. This is not a recommendation to remove useful features, shrink hit targets, hide navigation labels, or weaken television focus. Make resting controls quieter while preserving strong interaction feedback.

No P0 issues established. Two P1 priorities and three P2 improvements follow. This is an audit only; no application source changed. Builds, simulator runs, uploads, and deployment remain on hold.

## Priority findings

### P1 — Playback actions compete with the picture and change position

Evidence: `apple/App/Shared/PlaybackScreen.swift:326` shows Next episode whenever playback chrome is visible. Line 328 switches the contextual action group's bottom inset to 250 points when chrome appears. Lines 486–525 give Skip intro, Skip credits, and Next episode large white prominent button styling. Buttons also participate in the focused-control state that reveals chrome.

Impact: the prominent white group pulls attention away from the video. Its placement changes between hidden and visible chrome; actual focus loss has not been established. Routine Next episode has the same emphasis as a timely intro/credits action.

Recommendation: keep manual Next episode in the transport; elevate it near credits/end. Use a compact translucent resting treatment for contextual actions, with a strong focused/pressed state. Anchor the group consistently to measured transport and safe-area geometry rather than a fixed 250-point offset. Preserve labels and accessible target sizes. Suggested pass: quieter, then adapt.

### P1 — Playback settings present too many equally weighted choices

Evidence: `apple/App/Shared/PlaybackScreen.swift:2193` renders five bordered settings tabs; line 2220 begins Fit/Fill controls and four separate quality buttons. Subtitle timing has separate earlier/later/reset buttons at line 2402; custom caption styling exposes eight pickers from line 2423.

Impact: the settings drawer reads as a control inventory. Repeated borders and equivalent emphasis make current selections harder to scan and obscure the most common tasks.

Recommendation: use calm category navigation and labeled rows with current values. Put stream quality into one selection row, as playback speed already does. Keep timing grouped; put detailed caption appearance behind a disclosure. Reserve prominent treatment for an action that commits or starts playback. Suggested pass: distill, then quieter.

### P2 — The TV Requests toolbar crowds media and has a fixed layout risk

Evidence: `apple/App/tvOS/TVRequestsView.swift:106` combines a 52-point heading, fixed 440-point search field, two large bordered actions, spacing and 80-point horizontal insets in one HStack.

Impact: management controls compete with discovery content. Compression or focus-scale crowding is a risk requiring rendered validation, not a confirmed clipping failure.

Recommendation: use adaptive one-/two-row composition, compact secondary actions, and search as the clear entry point. Preserve TV focus targets. Suggested pass: adapt, then quieter.

### P2 — Secondary button treatment varies across sections

Evidence: `apple/App/Shared/NativeMusicLibraryView.swift:16` applies bordered button styling to the TV music shell; routine playlist commands appear at line 157. Requests uses large bordered secondary actions at `apple/App/tvOS/TVRequestsView.swift:151`. `apple/App/tvOS/TVRootView.swift:43` supplies a shared gold tint.

Impact: routine commands can look as important as Play. Repeated button containers compete with covers and backdrops.

Recommendation: align ordinary controls around a quiet resting style and distinct focus/press feedback. Keep gold primary emphasis selective. The existing secondary action style in `apple/App/Shared/MediaPresentation.swift:724` provides a useful starting vocabulary. Suggested pass: normalize, then quieter.

### P2 — Request hero copy adds generic hierarchy ahead of the title

Evidence: `apple/App/iOS/NativeRequestsView.swift:248` and `apple/App/tvOS/TVRequestsView.swift:397` include the generic “DISCOVER YOUR NEXT FAVORITE” eyebrow before title/logo and supporting metadata.

Impact: repeated promotional copy competes with the title artwork without helping viewers choose.

Recommendation: remove the generic eyebrow, or use that space only for meaningful availability/request status. Keep title/logo, concise metadata, and one primary action. Suggested pass: distill.

## What to preserve

- Shared billboard artwork, legibility gradients and title logos: `apple/App/Shared/MediaPresentation.swift:5` and `:96`. These support content rather than decorating the interface.
- Frameless TV posters with titles below artwork and focus tied to the artwork: `apple/App/tvOS/TVRootView.swift:852`. Historical gray title plates are not present in this current implementation.
- Mobile Home's restrained featured-media composition: `apple/App/iOS/MobileRootView.swift:1551`.
- Auto-hiding custom playback chrome and labeled focusable actions: `apple/App/Shared/PlaybackScreen.swift:291` and `:646`.
- Adaptive theme, existing touch targets, reduced-motion handling and visible TV focus. Quiet controls must remain discoverable and operable.

## Cognitive and audience assessment

For a viewer choosing a film, posters and title art already provide recognition; repeated promotion and button frames add noise. For someone watching on TV, contextual actions should appear where needed without changing position when focused. For mobile and low-vision users, lower visual emphasis must come from surface treatment and hierarchy, not smaller labels, smaller hit targets, or reduced contrast.

Main cognitive issue: too many controls receive equal emphasis. Progressive disclosure should retain every requested playback capability while reducing the number of simultaneous choices.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Quieter: playback contextual actions and ordinary secondary controls.
2. Distill: settings into current-value rows with advanced disclosure; remove generic hero copy.
3. Adapt: stable player action placement and responsive TV Requests toolbar.
4. Polish: spacing, typography, focus transitions and artwork legibility after those changes.

Once the user lifts the validation hold, check bright/dark video frames, long titles, compact layouts, VoiceOver, reduced motion, subtitle-heavy playback, and remote focus traversal. Until then, do not describe this audit as simulator- or device-verified.
